# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Kharcha is for Indian households and individuals who need a calm, dependable way to track everyday spending, income, and financial obligations without turning money management into a spreadsheet chore. The primary users are busy adults balancing personal and family finances on mobile-first workflows, logging in the middle of daily life rather than in a desk-bound planning session — often with only a few seconds and one hand free.

They range from confident money managers who want speed to lower-confidence users who want guidance rather than another intimidating finance tool. Kharcha has to serve both: fast for the fluent, forgiving for the unsure.

## Product Purpose

Kharcha brings expenses, income, and financial obligations into one place so users can understand where money is going, log transactions in seconds, and receive practical guidance that helps them make better decisions.

The product is built around three moves — **log it, see it, ask it**:

- **Log it.** Frictionless capture is the core loop. Users add a transaction by typing a plain sentence (parsed by AI into amount, category, payment mode, and date), by a quick-add drawer, or through a full form. Transactions carry type (income / expense / transfer), payment mode (cash / credit card), category, tags, counterparty, notes, and a recurring flag.
- **See it.** A dashboard surfaces net balance, income, and expenses, a category breakdown, spending over time, and recent activity — money made visual and legible at a glance, with amounts rendered in a monospaced numeric style so figures line up and read as data.
- **Ask it.** AI is a first-class decision layer, not a decorative one: a conversational assistant (Ask AI) answers questions about the user's own spending, and an AI Weekly Digest summarizes patterns and nudges. Guidance arrives before the next spending decision, not after.

Success looks like repeat use, frictionless entry, clear insight, and a product that feels trustworthy rather than intimidating.

**Direction (roadmap, not yet first-class in the data model):** shared / family accounts, EMI and obligation tracking, savings goals, and deeper insight surfaces (monthly comparison, spending anomalies). These are the product's stated ambition; today the schema is single-user, so design should leave room for them without implying they already exist.

## Brand Personality

Calm, capable, encouraging, and quietly intelligent. The experience should feel like a supportive financial co-pilot: steady, useful, and grounded rather than flashy or abstract. The AI voice is plainspoken and practical — a knowledgeable friend who explains, never a chatbot performing cleverness.

## Anti-references

Avoid cold fintech clichés, neon-heavy dashboards, overly clever AI gimmicks, glassmorphism-heavy visuals, spreadsheet-like density, and generic financial jargon that makes simple tasks feel intimidating. AI features especially must earn trust through usefulness and clarity, not sparkle — no gratuitous "magic," no confident-sounding advice the numbers don't support.

## Design Principles

- Make money feel understandable, not abstract.
- Reduce friction until logging feels effortless.
- Treat AI as a decision engine, not a decorative layer.
- Build trust with clarity, control, and visible feedback.
- Design for everyday family finance on small screens and fast-moving routines.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA-level clarity in color, contrast, interaction states, and keyboard support. The interface should support reduced motion, avoid color-only meaning, and remain usable on smaller screens and for lower-confidence users who need guidance rather than complexity. Currency and number formatting follow Indian conventions (₹, en-IN grouping).
